Honouring the Journey of Our Youth

Documenting VACFSS's annual youth transition ceremony — written and photographed on-site as Communications and PR Assistant.

Role Photography & Writing
Organization VACFSS
Year 2024

About this piece

Each year, VACFSS holds a ceremony honouring youth who are transitioning out of care — celebrating their resilience and connecting them to their cultural roots. As Communications and PR Assistant, I photographed the full event and wrote the published article covering the ceremony for the VACFSS website.

The challenge was capturing the weight and warmth of the occasion with sensitivity — documenting cultural ceremony, community, and personal milestones for an audience of stakeholders, families, and the broader public.

Youth wrapped in cultural blankets, gathered with community at the ceremony venue

The Ceremony

The event brought together youth transitioning out of care, their families, Elders, cultural knowledge keepers, and VACFSS staff. The ceremony included traditional drumming, dancing, the gifting of hand-painted cedar boxes, and the presentation of cultural blankets — each one a symbol of identity and belonging carried forward.

Cultural Gifts & Ceremony Details

Each youth received hand-painted cedar boxes and eagle feathers — gifts chosen to honour their journey and connect them to their heritage. The program materials, smudge bowl, and handmade items on display reflected the depth of preparation and cultural care that went into the event.

Community & Connection

What made this event powerful to document was the intergenerational presence — toddlers, Elders, youth, and community members all in the same space. The ceremony was as much about those who showed up as it was about those being honoured.

On documenting ceremony

Photography in this context is an act of care. Every shot involved reading the room — understanding when to be present and visible, and when to step back. The goal was to produce a record that the community would recognize as true to their experience, not a document made for outside eyes.

The published article was written the same way — grounded in what I observed and heard on the day, with language that honoured the significance of the milestone rather than flattening it into an announcement.
